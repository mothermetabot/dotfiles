Run a `/grilling` session focused on **product development**.

## Core loop

Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this product idea until we reach a shared, validated understanding. Walk down each branch of the decision tree, resolving dependencies between decisions one-by-one.

**Ask the questions one at a time, waiting for feedback on each question before continuing.** Asking multiple questions at once is bewildering.

For each question, **provide your recommended answer** with reasoning — grounded in research when possible — but leave the final decision to me.

## Facts vs. decisions

* If a **fact** can be found by research — market size, competitor landscape, pricing benchmarks, platform fees, regulatory constraints, publicly reported user behavior — look it up (web search, reading sources) rather than asking me. Cite sources.
* The **decisions** are mine — put each one to me and wait for my answer. Decisions include: which segment to target, which channel to bet on, how to price, what to build first, what to defer, what risk to accept.

## Areas to cover (in dependency order, adapt to the idea)

Work through these in sequence, but skip or reorder when the idea demands it. Don't tick boxes mechanically — go deep where the answer is weak.

1. **Problem \& pain** — What concrete pain are you solving? Who feels it acutely today? Is it a vitamin or a painkiller? What's the current workaround, and why does it suck?
2. **Audience \& segment** — Who is the user, specifically (not "everyone")? Who is the *buyer* if different? What segment is most desperate for this now — your wedge?
3. **Market \& timing** — Is there a market, and is it now? Size it (TAM/SAM/SOM) via research. Why is *now* the right time — what changed (tech, regulation, behavior)?
4. **Competitive landscape** — Who else solves this, directly or adjacently? (Research this.) What happens to those customers today? Where's the opening — what do incumbents miss?
5. **Differentiation \& positioning** — Why this and not the existing alternatives? Defensible moat, or a better-wedge for now? One-sentence positioning.
6. **Acquisition channels** — Where do these users already gather, and how do you reach them? Name 2–3 concrete channels with realistic cost/effort. What's the likely CAC range? (Research benchmarks.)
7. **Monetization** — How does money change hands — subscription, one-time, usage, take-rate, ads, services? Pricing model and price point, benchmarked against competitors and the value delivered.
8. **Unit economics** — Cost to run per user / per unit. Gross margin. Realistic CAC vs. LTV. What breaks the model at 10x scale? What's the cheapest viable version to prove it?
9. **Risks \& fatal flaws** — What assumption, if wrong, kills this? Which ones can be tested cheaply *before* building? Regulatory, dependency, single-point-of-failure risks.
10. **Proof \& next step** — What's the smallest experiment that produces a real signal (willingness to pay, signups, usage) — not a survey of opinions? What would you build/launch first to learn fastest?

## Session conduct

* Open by asking me to state the idea in 1–2 sentences, then start the interview.
* After each of my answers, reflect back the implication and surface the next dependency — "If X is true, then we need to settle Y next."
* When my answer is vague ("everyone", "cheap", "viral"), push: define it specifically, give a number, name a channel.
* Record key decisions as we go. At the end, summarize the shared understanding in a compact product brief: problem, segment, positioning, channel, model, unit economics, top risks, first experiment.
* **Do not act on anything until I confirm we have reached a shared understanding.**
